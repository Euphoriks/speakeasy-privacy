SpeakEasy Privacy Policy

Effective October 20, 2026

The short version: SpeakEasy turns your voice into text entirely on your phone. Your voice and what you dictate never leave your device. We don't run any servers, we don't use analytics or ads, and we don't have accounts.

What SpeakEasy does with your voice

When you tap the SpeakEasy bubble, the app records audio from your microphone (your phone's, or your connected earbuds' if you've allowed that) until you stop or pause. The audio is turned into text by a speech model that runs on your phone. The audio is never sent anywhere. It's kept only in your phone's memory while it's being transcribed, except when you cancel a dictation or it fails: then the recording is saved on your phone so you can retry it. Only the five most recent of those are kept, and you can delete them at any time by clearing your history.

Audio files you share to SpeakEasy

If you share a voice message or an audio or video file to "SpeakEasy: Transcribe", SpeakEasy reads only that file, the one you chose to share, and writes out what's said in it with the same on-phone speech model. The file is never sent anywhere. While it's being written out, its sound is kept in a temporary file in SpeakEasy's own storage on your phone, which is deleted as soon as it's done or stopped (or, if your phone closes SpeakEasy part way through, the next time SpeakEasy starts). The text is saved with your recent dictations, as described below, and the file itself is left as it was.

What SpeakEasy does with your text

The text is typed into the box you were using. Your recent dictations (up to 200) are saved on your phone so you can copy, search, retry or fix them, for as long as the "Keep dictations" setting says: Forever (the newest 200), 30 days, 7 days, or Off, which saves none. You can delete a single dictation or clear them all in the app at any time. For your stats on the Home screen (time saved, speaking speed, your streak, this week's words and achievements), it also keeps counts of the words you've dictated, how long you spoke and on which days, and how many words went into each app (numbers and app names only, never the words themselves; no app is noted for a banking or password app, a box the SpeakEasy Keyboard keeps out of History such as an Incognito tab, or while "Keep dictations" is Off; "Clear all" clears the app list too). These stay on your phone and are never backed up or sent anywhere. Your names list, corrections, snippets and settings are stored on your phone, and word lists are only exported when you choose "Export", to a location you pick. SpeakEasy's own backups are only made when you choose "Save to a file", to a location you pick. If your phone's Google backup is on, Android also includes your SpeakEasy settings, names list, corrections and snippets (not your history, recordings or models) in its backup to your Google account, so they come back on a new phone. When you dictate with the SpeakEasy Keyboard into a number or PIN box, a banking or password app (while "Hide in password fields and banking apps" is on), or a box the app marks as private (such as Chrome's Incognito tabs), the text is typed but not saved with your recent dictations. With "Show words as I speak: In the text box", a rough draft of your words appears in the box while you talk, so the app you're typing in can see it before you finish or cancel, just as it sees the final text.

The Accessibility service

SpeakEasy uses Android's Accessibility service to: notice when a keyboard is open so it can show the bubble, find the text box you're typing in so it can insert your words (and undo or polish them if you ask), check which app you're in so it can use the tone you chose for that app (for example, texting or email), and tap that app's Send button when you end a dictation by saying "send". It does not record your screen, read your messages, or collect anything you type yourself. Nothing it sees is stored or sent anywhere, apart from the dictation history described above.

The SpeakEasy Keyboard

If you turn on the optional SpeakEasy Keyboard, Android warns that keyboards can collect what you type. The SpeakEasy Keyboard only types what you dictate and the few keys it has (space, delete, enter). To add a space before your words and to undo a dictation, it looks at the text right before your cursor; it doesn't save or send that, or anything else you type.

Internet use

SpeakEasy uses the internet only to download speech and AI models when you ask it to, plus, on Wi-Fi only, a smaller helper speech model (about 148 MB, once per language) that shows your words as you speak on faster phones. These are downloaded from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), which, like any website, receives your IP address when you download a file. See Hugging Face's privacy policy. Nothing about you, your voice or your text is sent in these downloads.

Purchases

Subscriptions and the lifetime unlock are handled by Google Play. Google processes your payment under Google's privacy policy. SpeakEasy only checks with Google Play whether you've bought a plan; we never see your payment details.

Crash reports

If SpeakEasy crashes, a technical report is saved on your phone. It's only sent if you tap "Send report", which lets you choose how to share it (for example, by email). Reports contain technical details only, never your audio or anything you dictated.

Children

SpeakEasy is not directed at children under 13.

Deleting your data

Everything SpeakEasy stores is on your phone. Delete dictations or clear your history in the app, or uninstall SpeakEasy to remove all of it. If your phone's Google backup is on, Android's copy of your settings stays in your Google account until Android replaces or removes it (you can delete it in your Google account's backup settings).
